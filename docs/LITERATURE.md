# Literature and benchmark audit

The question is established, not an unsolved existence problem: minimizing truss material under stated stress and displacement limits has extensive prior work. The useful pilot objective is reproducible, strictly feasible discrete designs under one frozen model, with independent numerical checking. No worldwide best-known or global-optimum claim is made. Search checked primary papers, including later formulations, on 2026-10-20.

**Selection:** 10 bars give an easily audited planar example; 25 bars add spatial assembly, eight tied sizing groups and load redistribution. The 72-bar tower has 20 nodes, four 60-inch stories, a 120-inch square plan, 16 groups and two load combinations. Its surveyed continuous HBB-BC baseline and explicit geometry are in `72bar-survey.json`; only its mass is recomputed. It is not silently converted into a discrete or buckling-constrained problem. A future task must pin a discrete source and independently verify it first.

**Claims audit:** existence of established 10/25/72-bar benchmarks: confirmed by the cited sources. A unique universal version: contradicted by different catalogues, loads and displacement constraints. Kripka's 10-bar Table 1 method 5 mass: reproduced. The 25-bar Table 4 method 4 mass and constraints: reproduced and selected. Table 4 method 6: its printed vector gives 482.828644 lbm, about 1.501356 less than its reported 484.33, and a displacement utilization of 1.001716. Increasing group 5 from 2.1 to 2.2 would explain the mass and restore feasibility, but that is a hypothesis, not a correction to the source. The original vector remains a rejection fixture.

The 2012 Table 9 10-bar vector computes to 5479.937892 lbm but has displacement utilization 1.001946 in the pinned model; reject it at the pilot's strict tolerance. Possible original tolerance/model differences are not resolved here. The 72-bar table's lowest reported HS-CSS vector repeats earlier values in its last three entries; do not promote that claim without an audit. This leaves the paper's 379.66-lb HBB-BC column as a surveyed comparator, not a verified feasibility frontier.

**Settled cautions:** heuristic minima are not optimality proofs; area alone does not determine buckling inertia; stress feasibility does not establish elastic stability; equilibrium under one load can coexist with a mechanism; catalogue enlargement and topology relaxation invalidate direct historical improvement claims. Exhaustive reference spaces are 42^10 and 30^8. Kripka's prose prints 10^42 while its approximate count corresponds to 42^10.

**Frontier discipline:** the accepted publication baselines here mean “selected and recomputed,” not globally best. The local frontier table contains only author-checked results from this pilot. Physical safety and engineering novelty remain unestablished. Useful work includes source discrepancy resolution, externally implemented solver checks, topology stability verification and rigorous bounds for the exact frozen models.
